Alex Rivera, 72. Prefers to be called Alex. Lives in the Eastern time zone.
Uses one finger on an eyes-free drawing pad; right-hand motion is limited.
Jordan Chen (daughter) is the named caretaker. Tone on the pad should stay
calm and brief.

Recurring needs
- Water is the most frequent request, almost always after a morning walk or
  after time in the garden. A cup drawing means water, not tea, unless Alex
  has already said tea that day.
- Food is usually soup at lunch, oatmeal or toast in the morning, and a
  sandwich later. An apple or a pizza wedge is a specific ask, not generic
  food. Weekend evenings often mean pizza.
- Rest is a nap after lunch or lying down in the late afternoon. A bed
  drawing is rest, not a scene.
- Help is uncommon but urgent. A handwritten H, a plus, or a telephone
  means call Jordan. Do not treat the letter as the kind of help.

Company, not care
- Alex draws the garden, tomato vines, a sun over the back fence, and
  sometimes a bird on the feeder. Those are stories, not rest.
- A smile, a face, or a heart is talk: company, often thinking of Jordan
  or a neighbor who used to sit on the porch.
- Small scenes with a house and a tree are the old street. Offer a short
  story rather than fetching water.
- Music notes mean a story too — hymns and old radio more than a playlist.

Rhythms over the last two weeks
- Morning: walk if the weather is kind, then water, then oatmeal or toast.
  Garden days (tomatoes, sketches of leaves) cluster mid-morning.
- Jordan visits most afternoons. After a visit Alex sometimes draws a
  heart or a face and wants a few minutes of company on the pad.
- One afternoon Alex drew a telephone and confirmed a call; Jordan came
  over. After that, water and rest. No further help that week.
- A handwritten 1 or 2 starts the shape game (circle, square, triangle).
  Alex enjoys it when the hand is steadier, usually late morning.
- Evenings wind down with rest. A pizza drawing on Saturday was dinner,
  confirmed, not a triangle in the game.
